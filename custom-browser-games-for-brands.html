Custom Browser Games for Brands

An ad is glanced at and gone. A game, if it's fun, gets played to the end — then shared with friends. That is why brands use custom browser games for campaigns: people don't just see your message, they spend time inside it.

Why the browser format makes the most sense

A campaign game has to be playable that very second. The browser format meets that condition:

  • No install. One tap on a link from social media, WhatsApp, or a QR code on packaging or a booth — and the game runs. Every extra step (open the app store, download, register) throws away most of your potential players.
  • Runs on almost any phone. No need to think about your players' device specs; a light browser game opens even on a mid-range phone.
  • Easy to pass on. The same link can be forwarded by players to their groups. Score mechanics and friend challenges give people their own reason to spread it.
  • Your brand is inside the play. Logo, colours, products, and the campaign message become part of the arena, the characters, or the questions — not a sticker at the edge of the screen.

Game types that are realistic for a campaign

For a brand's needs, what works is actually a game that is simple and understood within seconds. A few formats proven to fit this scale:

  • Prize quiz. Questions about the product or campaign topic, scored on speed and accuracy. Fits product education and launches.
  • Short skill games. A simple race, an arena duel, or a rhythm game — one session takes one to three minutes, right for event booths and queues.
  • Product-assembly puzzle. Players assemble or match elements of your product; without noticing, they memorise its shape and name.
  • QR hunt. QR codes spread across an event venue or packaging, each opening a small challenge. Fits getting people moving in a physical place.

What should not be forced for a campaign: a big console-style game. Its build time and cost don't match a campaign's usually short run.

What you receive as the client

A tidy custom game order usually delivers: the game file ready to publish as a single web page (just put it at your address or share its link), a look that already uses your brand identity, your content (quiz questions, product lists, prize text) installed inside the game, and short instructions on how to share and run the campaign. Before ordering, prepare a picture of the mechanics you want, your brand materials (logo and colours), and the content that goes into the game — those three things decide how smoothly the build goes.

One thing to decide from the start: the prizes and how winners are determined. The game itself can be built, but the campaign rules — who may enter, how a top score is verified, when winners are announced — are the brand's decisions, and they're nicer to write before the game is finished than after.

A simple way to tell whether the campaign worked

You don't need complicated measurement tools to read a campaign game's results. Three basic numbers tell most of the story: how many people opened and actually played the game (not just clicked and left), how many finished a full session, and how many passed the link on to someone else. The first number tells you whether the invitation was attractive, the second whether the game itself was fun to play, and the third whether the mechanics were exciting enough to share voluntarily. If the second number is the weak one, the problem is in the game — sessions too long or rules too confusing — and that is concrete material to improve for the next campaign.

As for timing, the pattern brands commonly use divides it in three: a warm-up (teasers and a countdown on social media), the main run while the game is open and prizes are live, then a closing in the form of a winner announcement that doubles as content. Planning all three from the start makes one game work three times, not just once at launch.

Check the fit first

A good fit if:

  • You have a campaign moment — a launch, a promo, a giveaway, or an event booth.
  • Your audience is active on phones and likes sharing fun things.

Not yet a fit if:

  • You're picturing a long game or multiplayer — for campaigns, the short format always wins.
  • Prizes and winner rules aren't decided yet — decide them before the game is built.

Prepare before you order:

  • Brand materials: logo and colours.
  • The content going into the game (quiz questions, product list, prize text).
  • Where the link will be spread: posts, broadcasts, or a QR code.
